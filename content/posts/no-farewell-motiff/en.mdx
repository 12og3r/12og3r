---chapter: No Farewell, Motiff---

"Give me a bottle, give me a cigarette — I'll go when I go, I've got time. I don't want to spend the days ahead crying alone, unable to move on."

I'm leaving for Singapore soon. Next chapter. This isn't going to be a big retrospective post — just an ordinary goodbye, and some things I've been turning over in my head.

---chapter: the coach---

I talk every now and then with my life coach, Steven A. Martin. The conversation usually drifts back to the same question: what actually makes a life feel lived. He's told me his story in pieces. He ran away from home in Ohio at fifteen, made it to Hawaii alone, lived in a treehouse in the jungle. He's been a surf instructor, a chef, a guy at an astronomical station, a visiting scholar at Peking University, a professor. Once walked with his teacher from Xi'an to India, retracing Xuanzang's pilgrimage. For someone like me — raised on the standard Chinese trajectory — his résumé honestly stuns me. When I asked him what it all added up to, he just said it quietly: "A life shouldn't be lived inside a box."

---chapter: the box---

Steven's story cracked something open in me. I started looking harder at how I'm actually spending my time on this planet, which isn't unlimited. While I'm still young, while I still have some energy and runway, I want to figure out what life I actually want, instead of inheriting one. Unlock more of what's possible. Everybody calls me a shoe — but what if I want to be a purse?

I've been living inside a template my whole life. Hit the milestone, do the next thing, slide through to the end. After thinking about it for a while, I realized that's not the life I want. Or at least, not right now.

---chapter: not farewell---

"No Farewell, Motiff." I sat on this title for a while. In the end I didn't change it to "Goodbye."

Not because I had something clever to land. "Goodbye" just felt too heavy, too much like closing a book. What happened between me and this company, these people, doesn't close that way.

I can't always sort out which of the things I learned in these two years were technical and which were just about how to work with other humans. But I know this much: the standard for what "good" should look like, the reflex for how a thing ought to be built — most of that comes from here. That comes with me.

Going to Singapore isn't because I've figured anything out. Honestly, by the box logic, I'm just swapping one box for another. Bigger, stranger, still a box. The one thing that's different is I picked this one. First time.

I'm also under no illusion I'll become Steven. I'm not that wild, not that free. But I can at least stop every now and then and ask: am I doing this because I actually want to, or because the default carried me here? It's fine to take the default route in the end. As long as I noticed.

Back to Motiff. Calling it a goodbye is too heavy. The people, the code, the arguments we had — these will keep showing up in different forms. Different cities, different products, different names. We'll find each other.
